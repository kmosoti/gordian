# R6: context-construction headroom on the stream world

**Status: exploration.** Nothing here tests a hypothesis and nothing here may later be cited as
confirmation. Written by the R6 worker on branch `context-headroom`; the coordinator decides what
follows. This file is generated by `scripts/r6_assemble.py` from `scripts/r6-context-headroom.template.md`
and the committed CSVs, so every table cell comes from a file and none was typed. Numbers in the

## 1. Result in brief

1. **The criterion the coordinator fixed before any R6 run holds as written at the primary setting
   (b = 5, rho = 0.7) by both clauses, and at every sensitivity setting, and it holds for reasons
   that make it uninformative.** Clause 1: the privileged decisive-evidence context (R4's oracle)
   reaches 0.952 [0.933, 0.969] at 5.06 references per call; no public builder of the sweep has a
   context that small (the smallest, 8 references per call, scores 0.003), so the comparator is the
   empty context and the gap is the ceiling itself. Clause 2: no public
   builder at any size gets within 0.05 of 0.952 (the best, 0.796, is 0.156 below it), so the
   ratio of references is unreachable in every resample. Section 3.
2. **R4's oracle bundles a privilege the criterion does not name: timing.** It asks when all the
   decisive evidence has arrived; every public builder asks a fixed delay after notice. A labelled
   supplementary arm with the selection oracle's own anomalies and delay and the decisive evidence
   delivered by then as its context (`oracle_selection_context`) scores 0.820 [0.788, 0.851] at 5.26
   references per call. R4's oracle minus it is 0.132 [0.103, 0.162]: that is timing, and no context
   builder can supply it. Section 5.
3. **Simple public builders capture nearly all of the context lever in quality, and they pay for
   it in references.** With selection held at the selection oracle, the best builders reach 0.796
   (`window`, 40 s, at most 256 references, 251 references per call), 0.766 (`cooccur`, 16 s,
   188) and 0.763 (`neighbourhood`, 4 hops, 189), against the rung's own context 0.489 at 43
   references. The context-only ceiling minus the best public builder is 0.024 [0.000, 0.051];
   the best public builder minus the rung is 0.306 [0.265, 0.349]. To come within 0.05 of the
   context-only ceiling a public builder needs 33.1 [31.7, 49.5] times its references per call
   (174 against 5.26), and that ratio satisfies the plan's clause 2 on the supplementary reading
   at all six settings. Sections 4 and 5.
4. **What the builders fixed, from the re-trace (100 streams, 199 calls about hard incidents).** The
   rung's own context was complete in 77 calls; `window` (80 s) in 198, `window` (40 s) in 182,
   `cooccur` (16 s) in 166, `neighbourhood` (4 hops) in 167. Evidence at services the rung never
   attaches ("service not attached") is fixed: 217 of 235 observations are in the `cooccur` (16 s)
   and `neighbourhood` (4 hops) contexts, 218 in `window`'s (40 s) and 230 in `window`'s (80 s).
   "Outside window" is fixed in part: of the 102 observations at a dependent after the burst, 98
   are in every `cooccur` and `neighbourhood` context and 99 and 102 in `window`'s (40 s, 80 s); of
   the 59 that precede the anchor by more than 2 s (50 slow leak, 6 split brain, 3 compound) none is
   in a `cooccur` or `neighbourhood` context (they look back 2 s, the rung's own lookback), and
   36 and 56 are in `window`'s. Section 6.
5. **The pairings and the other columns.** `oracle_selection` with a builder costs 2.0 to 2.8
   modelled seconds a stream for 0.76 to 0.80, against 0.27 s for the context-only ceiling, 0.66 s
   for the rung's context (0.489), and 7.6 s for `always_escalate` with the rung (0.489). The
   realistic public pairing, `always_escalate` with a builder, tops out at 0.723 (`window`, 20 s,
   128 references, delay 16 s) for 17.4 s a stream, with 147 calls refused for the reasoner's token
   budget; with larger contexts the budget refuses up to 1153 calls in 200 streams. Plain accuracy
   and critical misses are in every table. Sections 4 and 7.
6. **Recommendation (section 10).** EXP-102 as designed (a learned or constructed context against
   simple ones) has 0.024 [0.000, 0.051] of quality to win over the best simple builder at the
   primary setting and 33 times the references; it should claim references per call at matched
   quality, with timing and the slow-leak family as separate, named levers. The substrate
   prototype's first job, on this evidence, is not binding evidence across services and time
   (simple builders do that) but deciding when the evidence is ready and which few of the
   hundreds of references a builder returns are the evidence. Both depend on one assumption of
   the simulated reasoner that this work did not test (section 10).

## 2. What was run

**Code (commit `1ee8fa6`, before any run).** The context of an escalation is built by the shared
rung, so a *context builder* is an option of the rung, named per arm in the manifest
(`arms[].context`) or for every arm (`rung.context`). Four exist (`src/stream/arms/context.rs`):

- `rung`: the rung's own context, the default, unchanged and not written to a manifest;
- `window`: every held observation of the last `W` seconds before the call, at any service, benign
  and abnormal alike, the `N` most recent, most recent first;
- `cooccur`: held observations from 2 s before the anomaly's anchor to the call, at the anomaly's
  site and at every service whose abnormal readings *began* within `delta` of the anchor (a
  beginning is an abnormal observation not preceded at its service by another within the rung's
  2 s burst gap), capped at `N` by keeping the first quarter and the most recent rest, as the
  rung does;
- `neighbourhood`: held observations from 2 s before the anchor to the call, at services within
  `k` hops of the site in the public dependency graph (edges either way), capped likewise.

Each is a pure function of a `PublicView` (the observations the rung holds, the public graph, the
instant of the latest step, the anomaly's anchor instant and site) and nothing else. The module is
under `arms/`, which `scripts/check-no-oracle.sh` and `tests/stream_arms.rs` ban from naming any
hidden type; a test repeats the ban for the file by name and pins the view's seven fields.
`oracle_selection` takes any builder through its rung with no change of its own, and its only
privilege is still which anomalies are hard.

**Tests (`tests/stream_context.rs`, 17).** Each builder against a hand-built store and graph; each
deterministic, within its cap, repeating no reference and asking about held observations only
(`escalations_refused` is 0 and the cap binds in the test, so the check has power); contexts the
same under three reasoner settings (`b`, `rho` are hidden parameters); the selection oracle with
each builder asks exactly where `always_escalate` asks about hard incidents with the same contexts;
`rung`, defaulted or spelled per arm or for the whole rung, writes the `results.csv` and
`incidents.csv` that the binary of `1b7b0c7` (before R6) wrote, byte for byte, for four arms that
escalate (fixtures `tests/fixtures/stream-r6-regression`, written by that binary), and a manifest
written before R6 serializes to the same text; a non-default builder changes the results of every
arm that escalates. All gates exit 0 (section 9).

**One addition the plan did not list.** `oracle_selection_context`, a privileged arm named and
labelled as supplementary: the selection oracle's anomalies and delay, with the decisive evidence
delivered by then as the context. It exists because R5 flagged that R4's oracle bundles selection,
timing and context and R6's ceiling is that oracle. It is not used by the criterion's verdict; it
gives a second reading, and the split in section 5.

**Settings and seeds.** R4's six reasoner settings (b in {2.5, 5, 8} by rho in {0, 0.7}). Seeds as
the plan fixes them: tuning 10000-10099, held-out 20000-20199; the re-trace uses 31000-31099
(not R5's 30000-30099, not a tuning or held-out seed). The held-out world is the same in all six
settings (5316 incidents with identical tier, family and criticality; 511 hard incidents, 372
outside the slow-leak family, 139 slow leak).

**Quality, cost and references.** As in R4 and R5: quality is the pooled fraction (sum over
streams) of hard incidents outside the slow-leak family declared correctly by their deadline; cost
is the mean modelled total cost per stream (substrate and rule plus reasoner); references per call is
the pooled sum of references over the pooled sum of calls (the plan's criterion measures it); the
intervals are 90% equal-tailed percentile intervals from 10,000 resamples of whole streams.
The builder's own computation is not in the modelled cost (the rung's context construction never
was); the measured scheduling time of every builder arm at the primary setting is within 3% of
`never_escalate`'s (9.0 to 9.3 ms a stream), so it is not hidden cost of any size.

**Tuning, on the tuning streams only** (`r6_select.py`, rules fixed before any run):

1. The selection oracle at the delay R5 tuned for the setting (not re-tuned) with every builder
   configuration of the grid: `window` W in {5, 10, 20, 40, 80} s by N in {8, 16, 32, 64, 128,
   256, 512} (35); `cooccur` delta in {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16} s by N in {64, 128, 256} (21);
   `neighbourhood` k in {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6} by N in {64, 128, 256} (18); the rung. The grids are
   generic geometric ladders. **The grid was widened after a 10-stream pilot on tuning seeds**
   (`r6-pilot-b5-rho0.7`): the rung's cap of 128 truncated the larger `cooccur` and `neighbourhood`
   contexts, so their cap was swept too (a superset of the plan's W x N, delta and k, more generous to
   the public builders), and window's N went to 512. No held-out stream was touched.
2. Per builder kind, the configurations on its frontier of tuning quality against references per
   call (exact ties on both axes count once). 25 to 29 configurations a setting were carried,
   the rung always.
3. For each carried configuration, `always_escalate`'s delay over R4's 13-delay grid: the delay of
   highest tuning quality, the cheapest cost of ties.

## 3. The criterion as written

> EXP-102 has context-construction headroom if, at the primary setting (b = 5, rho = 0.7), with
> selection held at the selection oracle, either
> 1. the privileged decisive-evidence context exceeds the best public builder by at least 0.10 in
>    hard-incident quality (slow leak excluded) at equal or fewer references per call, with the
>    90% cluster-bootstrap lower bound above 0.05; or
> 2. to come within 0.05 of the ceiling's quality, the best public builder needs at least 1.5 times
>    the references per call, with the 90% interval of that ratio excluding 1.25.
>
> Critical misses and plain-incident accuracy are reported beside quality for every row and are
> never folded into it. The same numbers are reported at b = 2.5 and b = 8.

**Readings the plan leaves open, fixed in `r6_stats.criterion` before any held-out number was
seen, and stated so that they can be challenged:**

- The ceiling is R4's `oracle_escalation_privileged` ("R4's oracle remains the decisive-evidence
  ceiling", as the task states).
- "Public builders" are the selection-oracle arms of the carried configurations of `window`,
  `cooccur`, `neighbourhood` and the rung. The `always_escalate` pairings are in section 7.
- Clause 1: the best held-out quality among those arms whose references per call do not exceed the
  ceiling's, recomputed inside every resample; the empty context (quality 0) is the comparator
  when none qualifies, as R5's `never_escalate` was for cost.
- Clause 2: "within 0.05" is quality at least the ceiling's minus 0.05; "the best builder" is the
  one with the fewest references per call of those; the ratio is its references per call over the
  ceiling's, infinite when none reaches that quality; "excluding 1.25" is a 5th-percentile ratio
  above 1.25 with the point estimate at least 1.5.
- b = 2.5 and b = 8 are at rho = 0.7 (only b varies); rho = 0 is shown beside them.

{{criterion}}

The verdict as written: **both clauses hold at the primary setting and at every sensitivity
setting.** The numbers, at the primary setting: clause 1 gap 0.952 [0.933, 0.969] against a margin of
0.10 and a lower bound of 0.05; clause 2 ratio unreachable, in all 10,000 resamples, against 1.5
and 1.25. At b = 2.5 (rho = 0.7) the gap is 0.538 [0.494, 0.582] and the ratio is unreachable in
85% of resamples with a 5th percentile of 62.5; at b = 8 the gap is 1.000.

**Why this verdict says little, and what I think it does say.** Both clauses are satisfied by
construction here and not by the builders' performance:

- Clause 1 asks for a public builder at 5.06 references per call or fewer. A public builder does
  not know which handful of observations is the evidence; at 8 references it scores 0.003, at 16
  0.043, at 32 0.110 (section 4, `window`). The comparator is therefore the empty context, and the
  gap is the ceiling's whole quality. A criterion that compares at "equal or fewer references" the
  ceiling's few references is met by any ceiling that clears the margin over the empty context.
- Clause 2 is unreachable because R4's oracle's 0.952 is above anything a public builder reaches at
  any size (best 0.796), for the reason in section 5: the oracle asks at an instant no public
  builder is given.

What the verdict does say: there is a large gap in references per call between public contexts and
the decisive-evidence context, and a gap in quality between public contexts and R4's oracle. The
supplementary reading below separates the two.

the selection oracle with the rung and with the highest-quality configuration of each builder on

Each one missing from a builder's context is `context cap` (the builder admits it and the cap
dropped it) or `not admitted` (the builder's rule excludes it).

## 8. Slow leak

The slow-leak family is outside the quality figures as in R4 and R5. Its rates are in section 7:
the selection oracle with the rung scores 0.19, with a builder 0.09 to 0.30 (the best, 0.30, is
`window` 80 s; `window` 20 s is the lowest), the context-only ceiling 0.28 and R4's oracle 0.91. The re-trace explains the first
step (the evidence precedes the anchor by more than the 2 s lookback, so only a long `window`
carries it) and the second (the arms call only about anomalies the rung noticed). It does not
explain the third: the difference between 0.28 and 0.91 is the oracle's calling without a noticed
anomaly, which is a salience privilege no builder or timing rule here supplies.

## 10. Recommendation, uncertainty, and next experiments

These are my judgements and not part of the criterion; the coordinator decides.

**For EXP-102.**

1. **State the claim as references per call (and cost) at matched hard-incident quality, with the
   best public builder as the baseline.** At the primary setting the best public builder is
   within 0.024 [0.000, 0.051] of the context-only ceiling, so a constructed context cannot win
   0.10 of quality over it; it can win the references (33 times at the plateau) and therefore most
   of the cost of the call (8 to 11 times at the plateau: 2.0 to 2.8 s a stream against 0.27 s).
2. **Separate timing and the slow-leak family from context in the registration.** R4's oracle's
   advantage over the context-only ceiling is 0.132 and the leak is 0.28 against 0.91; neither is
   context construction. A preregistration that quotes R4's oracle as the ceiling for a context
   builder will find the ceiling unreachable (as clause 2 above is) and will say nothing.
3. **The baselines are `window`, `cooccur` and `neighbourhood`** tuned as here (or by the
   frontier method, since a single tuned point would understate them) with the selection oracle;
   `window` 20 s with N 256 (0.774 at 174 references) and `cooccur` 4 to 16 s are the strong cheap
   ones.
4. **Bound critical misses and plain accuracy** as R5 asked; the realistic pairing lowers plain
   accuracy to 0.756 with the window builders.

**For the substrate prototype's first job.** R5's note was that if simple builders capture the
lever, the first job is not binding. They do capture it, in quality. What is left, on this
evidence, in order of size at the primary setting:

- *Readiness* (0.132 [0.103, 0.162] of quality, and the leak): knowing when the decisive evidence has
  arrived. The public builders use a fixed delay after notice; R5's tuned delay is flat from 14 to 18 s.
- *Compaction* (33 times the references, 8 to 11 times the cost per stream): choosing the few
  references that carry the evidence out of the 170 to 250 a builder returns. Whether public
  information discriminates the evidence is **not measured**: this work did not look at which
  references a compaction would keep, and the stream's evidence may be built so that no public
  feature marks it.
- *Selection* (R5: the cost of 25 calls instead of 2).

I would give the first job to readiness-and-compaction together: given an anomaly and a builder's
broad context, decide when to ask and what to keep, scored by quality against references per call and
timing, against `window` and `cooccur` as the baselines. I would not give it binding.

**Least certain.**

- **The reasoner's context law.** DESIGN.md section 11: the answer depends on the truth only through
  the decisive evidence in the context, `q`, and extra references "cost but never hurt". Under that
  law a broad context loses nothing but tokens, which is what makes `window` competitive. If a
  real reasoner is hurt by distractors (EXP-106 is where that is tested), every public builder's
  plateau falls and the references a builder needs to hand over fall with it; the headroom here
  could be larger, and the ranking of builders could change. R6 swept `(b, rho)` and not the cost per
  reference or a distractor penalty. The 33-fold ratio is a ratio of reference counts at the stream's
  declared price of 20 tokens each.
- **The comparison among builders at the plateau.** 0.763 to 0.796 on 372 incidents: not distinct. The
  grids are coarse ladders; a combination (a `window` over the `neighbourhood`, a longer lookback for
  `cooccur`) was not built and could matter, in particular for the slow leak.
- **The delay.** The selection oracle uses R5's tuned delay for the rung's context; a builder's
  context may be better at another delay. The `always_escalate` pairings were tuned per
  configuration and landed mostly at 14 to 18 s, which is some evidence the delay is not
  binding.
- **The re-trace** covers one setting and 100 streams.
- **What I read, and when.** In scanning `gordian-stream/DESIGN.md` for its public sections I read
  sections 4, 5 and 11 as well, which describe the hidden rules of the hard families, contrary to the
  instruction to read the public sections only. The builder definitions are the plan's; their
  parameters are tuned on tuning streams; the grids are generic ladders in the rung's own scales
  (its 400 ms burst, 2 s gap and lookback, 120 s retention) written before any run. But a grid
  that begins at 0.25 s and a lookback of 2 s were chosen by someone who had read that a partner
  service alarms 20 to 230 ms after the first alarm and that phase-2 evidence arrives 6 to 16 s
  after onset. I do not think the builders encode that knowledge (each is a pure function of a
  public view, tested), and the `window` builder, which encodes no timing at all, is the best, but
  the coordinator should weigh that I cannot rule out the influence of what I had read.

**Rejected or not done.** I did not change the criterion, its margins or its readings after seeing
a number; the readings are in section 3, written into `r6_stats.py` before the held-out runs. I did not tune
on a held-out stream. I did not use R4's oracle as anything but the ceiling the task named, and the
supplementary ceiling is labelled. I did not build combinations of builders, a learned builder, a
compaction, or a reasoner with a distractor penalty. I did not replay R4's manifests (R5 did, and the
R5 cross-check above chains to it).

**Suspected defects.** None in the code that I found: the tests pass, the cross-check is byte-exact and
the re-trace's independent implementation agrees on every call. Design defects in the criterion, for
the coordinator: (1) clause 1's "equal or fewer references per call" is infeasible for any public
builder, so its comparator is the empty context and the clause is a statement about the ceiling
alone; (2) clause 2 inherits R4's oracle's timing privilege, which makes its ratio unreachable. A
criterion for EXP-102 should name its ceiling (context-only) and compare at matched quality.
Smaller points: `cooccur` and `neighbourhood` share the rung's cap policy (first quarter, most recent rest)
while `window` keeps the most recent, so their caps lose different observations (section 6); the
`oracle_selection_context` arm makes one call more than the selection oracle in the 200 streams of
the primary setting (421 against 420): in seed 20167 it noticed one more anomaly (`anomalies_noticed`
is 1 higher; hard incidents escalated are equal in all 200 streams). I did not trace why. The arms differ only in that
this one asks by direct request, which records no attempt on the anomaly view; that is a hypothesis
for the difference, not a finding, and it does not change what the arm asks about.

**Smallest high-information next steps.** (1) A reasoner with a distractor penalty (and a
cost-per-reference sweep) at the primary setting, to see whether the plateau, the ranking and the
33-fold ratio survive; this decides whether R6's finding is about the world or about the reasoner's
law. (2) A public compaction of the `window` context (drop benign and repeated readings, keep one
per service and kind, say) measured as references per call at matched quality: the cheapest test of
whether compaction has anything public to work with. (3) A readiness signal (quiescence of new
abnormal observations after notice) against the fixed delay, measured against the 0.132.
