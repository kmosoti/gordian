# R5: decomposed headroom on the stream world

**Status: exploration.** Nothing here tests a hypothesis and nothing here may later be cited as
confirmation. Written by the R5 worker on branch `decomposed-headroom`; the coordinator decides what
follows. This file is generated by `scripts/r5_assemble.py` from `scripts/r5-decomposed-headroom.template.md`
and the committed CSVs, so every table cell comes from a file and none was typed. Numbers in the
prose were typed by the worker from those tables and the scripts' printed output and were checked
against them; a reader should do the same for any that matter.

R5 was begun by one worker, interrupted by a container restart before its last regression replay,
and finished by a second worker (this one). Section 9 says exactly what each did.

## 1. Result in brief

1. **The criterion the coordinator fixed before any R5 run holds at the primary setting
   (b = 5, rho = 0.7), by both of its clauses, and at every sensitivity setting.** The selection
   oracle exceeds the best non-privileged configuration at equal or lower cost by 0.433 in hard
   incident quality (slow leak excluded), 90% cluster-bootstrap interval [0.389, 0.476], against a
   margin of 0.10 and a lower bound of 0.05 (clause 1). The cheapest public configuration within
   0.05 of the selection oracle's quality costs 10.75 times as much, interval [9.08, 11.79], against
   1.5 and an interval that must exclude 1.25 (clause 2). Section 3.
2. **The headroom is in cost, and not in quality.** The best public configuration of any cost
   reaches the selection oracle's quality: the difference is 0.000 [-0.035, 0.033] at the primary
   setting (`always_escalate` with a 14 s delay, 0.489, at 7.36 modelled s a stream, against the
   selection oracle's 0.489 at 0.66 s). Clause 1 holds because the cost cap at 0.66 s leaves public
   arms about one call a stream; clause 2 holds because escalating everything costs about 11 times
   what escalating only the hard anomalies costs. "Selection headroom" here means "the same hard
   incident quality for roughly a tenth of the cost", which is what the plan's two clauses measure.
   It does not mean the selection oracle is better than any public configuration at any price.
3. **`contradiction_escalation`, the strongest public-information baseline, did not close any of
   it.** Its best tuned configuration reaches 0.478 at 7.86 s (primary setting): about
   `always_escalate`'s point, at the same cost. The checker contradicts almost every anomaly at some
   point (97% of the 1931 plain anomalies on the tuning streams), so the signal does not select.
   Section 5 and `r5-contradiction-probe.txt`.
4. **Most of R4's oracle quality is not selection.** At the primary setting the R4 oracle reaches
   0.952 and the selection oracle 0.489; the difference, 0.462 [0.418, 0.506], is timing and context.
   The trace explains most of it: for the two burst families the context the cheap rung builds
   lacked decisive evidence that had already arrived in 290 of 293 delayed calls about hard
   incidents (cascade and split-brain, summed over the three delayed arms traced), almost always because the evidence was at a
   service the rung does not attach. That deficit is shared by every arm that uses the rung's
   context, so selection cannot repair it. Section 6.
5. **The decoy oracle matters on false alarms, not on cost.** It dismisses 352 of 541 decoys at
   zero reasoner cost and lowers false alarms by 1.88 a stream [1.68, 2.08] against `never_escalate`;
   every public arm that escalates raises them (0.52 [0.43, 0.60] more with the best public arm).
   Section 7.
6. **Recommendation (section 10):** the criterion is met; EXP-101 may be preregistered, with its
   primary comparison stated as cost at matched hard-incident quality, not as quality, with the
   limits in sections 8 and 10 written into it. Whether to run context construction (EXP-102)
   first is the coordinator's decision and is not forced by this result.

**Held-out runs.** One run per setting, 41 to 60 arms interleaved over the 200 streams: R4's

equal-tailed percentile intervals from 10,000 resamples of whole streams. Plain accuracy,
critical misses and false alarms are reported beside quality and never folded into it.

## 5. The new public arm and the selection signal

### 5.1 `contradiction_escalation`

Per setting, its best tuning configuration and frontier size are in the table of section 2; on the
held-out streams its best point at the primary setting is `contra_d18_p02`, 0.478 at 7.86 s with
23.5 calls a stream, against `always_d14`'s 0.489 at 7.36 s with 24.7. At no setting is a
contradiction configuration more than about 0.3 s (4%) cheaper than the cheapest
`always_escalate` that reaches its quality, and the cheapest contradiction point costs 6.3 s.
**It is not a selective rule.** `r5-contradiction-probe.txt` (tuning streams, committed) counts,
per tier and family, anomalies, those the public checker finds contradicted at notice, those ever
contradicted, and those still contradicted when only the incident's own evidence is kept:

- plain: 1931 anomalies, 870 contradicted at notice (45%), 1881 ever (97%), 224 (12%) on their own
  evidence;
- hard compound: 61, 45, 59, 58 (95% on their own evidence); hard cascade: 72, 29, 71, 11; hard
  split-brain: 66, 34, 64, 2; hard slow leak: 18, 9, 18, 0.

So the checker contradicts nearly everything, mostly because an anomaly's attached evidence
includes other incidents' observations; only for the compound family does the contradiction
reflect the incident itself. The persistence requirement, tuned like every baseline, does not
repair this. This is a finding about this checker and this world; a more careful public cascade
(a different checker, a different uncertainty) is not excluded by it.

### 5.2 What a public selector would have to do

The selection oracle makes 2.1 calls a stream (it escalates 78% of hard incidents, those whose
anomaly was noticed), the best public arm 24.7. The gap to close is therefore the choice of anomalies, and R5 found no public
signal (checker contradiction, score thresholds, change, periodic review, random) that makes it.

## 6. Trace: why the context lacks decisive evidence that has arrived

Diagnostic run, primary setting, 100 streams (30000-30099), every ledger kept, arms
`always_d06`, `always_d14`, `contra_d18_p02`, `oracle_selection_d16_privileged` (the R4 oracle is
excluded: its context is the evidence by construction). For every accepted escalation about a hard
incident (the incident of the focus observation, read from the hidden labels the way the evaluator
reads them), the decisive observations emitted no later than the call's step are "arrived"; a call
is **complete** if its context holds all of them, **before evidence** if none had arrived, and
otherwise **lacking**. Each missing observation is classified, in order: `service not attached` (its
service is neither the anomaly's site nor a dependent in the public graph), `outside window` (older
than the rung's 2 s lookback before the anomaly's anchor, or at a dependent more than 400 ms after
it), `context cap` (the rung would admit it but the 128-reference cap dropped it), `other`.

**Validation of the classifier.** Its rule is the rung's own; for every traced call it predicts
the whole context and compares with what the ledger recorded: 196 of 196, 181 of 181, 176 of 176
and 180 of 180 match (script output). So "other" being empty means the rule explains every missing
observation, not that it was left unexamined.

{{trace}}

Reading the rows for the three delayed arms (the `ALL ARMS` rows pool `always_d06` too, whose calls
are early):

- **compound** calls are complete in nearly every case (55 of 57, 54 of 56, 54 of 56): the rung's
  context suits that family.
- **cascade and split-brain** calls are almost never complete (0 or 1 of about 49 each, per arm):
  the evidence sits at services the rung does not attach (most missing observations), or is older
  or later than the windows. This is the same for every arm, because every arm uses the rung's
  context, so `always_escalate`, `contradiction_escalation` and the selection oracle all send the
  reasoner about the same incomplete evidence.
- **slow leak**: 43 missing observations per arm, all outside the window (the leak starts well
  before the 2 s lookback); the shared rung never notices the family (section 8).
- `context cap` and `other`: zero in every row.

R4 reported that `always_escalate`'s contexts held none of the decisive evidence in 17 of 22
burst incidents at notice and were complete in 22 of 66 with a 14 s delay; this trace gives the
reason: not evidence outside a window or a full context, mostly evidence at a service the rung
never attaches.

**What this does and does not say.** It says why the selection oracle's quality is 0.489 and not
0.952: the context lever is the larger one at b = 5, and no selection rule affects it. It does not
measure how much quality a better context would add (that is EXP-102's question; R4's arithmetic
put it at roughly half), and it covers one setting and 100 streams.

## 10. Recommendation, uncertainty, and next experiments

**The criterion is met at the primary setting by both clauses, and at b = 2.5 and 8.** I recommend
that EXP-101 may be preregistered, subject to the following, which are my judgements and not part
of the criterion:

1. **State the claim as cost at matched hard-incident quality.** The selection oracle does not beat
   the best public arm of any cost on quality (-0.043 to 0.000 across the six settings, intervals
   containing zero or adjacent to it). A preregistered comparison that asks a learned selector to
   beat public arms on quality at equal cost is asking for the ratio of 10 that clause 2 measured;
   one that asks it to beat them on quality at any cost is asking for something the ceiling does
   not offer.
2. **Say what the ceiling is.** A selector's quality ceiling with the rung's context is the
   selection oracle's, 0.489 at b = 5 and 0.220 to 0.247 at b = 2.5; the R4 oracle's 0.952 includes
   context construction, which is EXP-102. With the rung's context the cascade and split-brain
   families are almost out of reach for any selector.
3. **Decide the plain-incident treatment before freezing.** `always_escalate` buys 0.074 plain
   accuracy and 26 fewer critical misses at the primary setting for its cost; the criterion does not
   count them.
4. **Public signals found so far do not select.** If EXP-101's selector is to be learned, it needs
   features the checker, the score and the timing rules do not provide; R5 has not shown which, only
   that the obvious public one does not.
5. **Whether to run EXP-102 first is the coordinator's call.** R5 neither requires nor excludes it.
   The trace and the decomposition say the larger quality lever at b = 5 is context, and the
   criterion says selection still has room in cost.

**Least certain.**

- The cost ratio (10.75) is mostly a ratio of call counts (24.7 against 2.1 a stream, and the
  substrate cost of a no-call rung is a fixed 0.44 s). R5 swept the reasoner's `b` and `rho` and
  did not sweep its cost per call. A cheaper reasoner shrinks the ratio, and I did not measure by
  how much.
- Held-out runs contain only the configurations on the tuning frontiers (as in R4); a better
  public configuration off those frontiers is possible. The tuning grids are coarse at the cheap
  end (random p of 0.03 and 0.06 are the lowest). The best affordable public arm has quality
  at most 0.06, and I do not think a finer grid changes that, but I did not test it.
- The trace covers one setting, 100 streams and three delayed arms, and classifies by the rung's
  own rule re-derived from the public graph; the 100% context-prediction match supports the
  classifier, not the generalisation to other settings.
- The 32-against-32 tie: at the primary setting `always_d14` and the selection oracle at 16 s both
  score 182 of 372 with 64 incidents on which exactly one is right (32 each way). I checked it:
  the two make one call per hard incident with different contexts (a different delay changes what
  has arrived), which gives an independent draw. Quality as a function of delay is flat between 14
  and 18 s (0.489, 0.489, 0.500), so a tie is expected. It is not evidence of a shared mechanism
  beyond that.

**Rejected or not done.** I did not change the criterion, its margins or its readings after seeing
a number; the readings are in section 3. I did not use the hidden-rules ablation as a baseline. I
did not tune anything on a held-out stream. I did not separate timing from context in the R4
oracle (a fourth arm would be needed and the plan does not ask for it).

**Smallest high-information next steps.** (1) A reasoner-cost sweep at the primary setting for
the selection oracle against `always_escalate` (the one number the ratio most depends on).
(2) A context-only privileged arm (public selection, privileged context) to size EXP-102 against
EXP-101 directly. (3) A public selector with a feature set that discriminates hard from plain
anomalies, measured against the 0.66 s selection ceiling.
