# A7b: calibration of the relative-savings interval

Status: development run, `ratio-interval` branch. Nothing here tests a hypothesis and nothing here
may later be cited as confirmation. It measures an instrument, the interval that decides EXP-001's
cost criterion, on B1's cost distribution. The plan item is `docs/local-test-plan.md` 5/A7b; the
inputs are the A7 review entry in `docs/review-log.md` and B2's size check (`b2-power.md`, T5).

## 1. Result

1. **Default: the studentized interval** (bootstrap-t on the log ratio of totals), selectable with
   `gordian-analyze compare --relative-savings --interval-method {percentile,bca,studentized}`.
   BCa and the original percentile interval stay available.
2. **Acceptance (false exceedance at true S = 0.20, planned n, at most 0.06 over at least 2,000
   experiments, B1's empirical costs): met by the default, in all eight cells** (four empirical
   populations, n = 1,237 and 1,713, 2,000 experiments each, 10,000 bootstrap resamples). The
   largest rate is 0.0545 (P1 at 60,000, n = 1,713). That cell was then extended to 10,000
   experiments: 0.0484 (se 0.0021, 95% upper bound 0.0528).
3. **The 0.06 bar is a point-estimate bar at this experiment count.** With 2,000 experiments the
   Monte Carlo standard error is 0.005, so in three of the eight cells the 95% upper bound of the
   default's rate is above 0.06 (Table 2). The evidence for "at most 0.06" is that all eight
   point estimates are between 0.047 and 0.0545, their mean is 0.0498, and the one cell that came
   closest, re-run on 8,000 further experiments, fell to 0.0484. It is not a proof for each cell.
4. **The original percentile interval does not meet the bar as specified**: 0.064 at P1 60,000,
   n = 1,713 in the same 2,000 experiments (its rate over the extended 10,000 is 0.0564). Its
   problem is below the planning sizes: at n = 40 to 160 it reaches 0.117 and 0.078.
5. **Not every distribution passes.** On heavy-tailed lognormal costs (sigma_A = 1.0; much more
   skewed than anything in B1) every method exceeds 0.06 at both planning sizes; the default
   is the closest (0.072 and 0.065). B1's costs are not that skewed (Table 1), but the
   instrument's size depends on the shape of the cost distribution, and the selective arm's
   shape is not known yet.
6. **Power** of the default at true S = 0.25 and 0.30 is 1.000 (no miss in 2,000 experiments, so
   at least 0.9985 at 95% confidence) at n = 1,237 and 1,713 in all four empirical populations
   (Table 5), and 0.79 to 0.99 on the heavy lognormal. The cost criterion is not the binding one
   at the planning sizes, as B2 already found. At n = 40 the default has much less power than the
   percentile interval on the most skewed population (0.16 against 0.61 at S = 0.25); the percentile interval's figure includes
   its excess false rejections there (size 0.117), so it is not a like-for-like sensitivity.

## 4. The null, and the populations

**Empirical null.** A simulated experiment draws n of the 5,500 paired episodes of one proxy pair
at one compute level, with replacement (the class mix therefore varies from experiment to
experiment, as in B2's simulation), after arm B has been multiplied by one constant so that
sum(B)/sum(A) over the 5,500 is exactly 0.80: the true S is 0.20 and every episode's cost
relative to its partner, and its covariance with A, is B1's. The same construction with 0.75 and
0.70 gives the power runs (true S = 0.25 and 0.30).

**Lognormal comparison.** A_i = exp(sigma_A Z), B_i = A_i rho_i with rho_i = (1 - S) exp(sigma_r Z' - sigma_r^2/2),
independent of A, so E[B]/E[A] = 1 - S exactly. Two settings, my choice because A7's parameters
are not in the repository: *LN-mild* (sigma_A = 0.5, sigma_r = 0.25) and *LN-heavy* (sigma_A = 1.0,
sigma_r = 0.5).

spread sets the interval (sigma_1 is B2's `sd(z)/mean(A)`).

"Observed S" is of the original pairs before rescaling. Runs per cell: **2,000 experiments,
10,000 bootstrap resamples** (the CLI default), 90% two-sided interval (alpha = 0.05 per tail), the
decision "lower limit > 0.20". Every experiment has its own stream,
`SeedSequence(20261005, spawn_key=(population id, round(1000 S), n, experiment index))`; population
ids 10 to 13 are the four empirical populations in the order of Table 1, 20 and 21 the lognormals.
numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1, Python 3.11.15. Rates use the same experiments for all three methods
(they read the same resamples), so differences between methods are paired.

## 5. The three methods

- **percentile**: the 5th and 95th percentiles of the resampled S (the A7 interval, unchanged).
- **BCa**: Efron's bias-corrected and accelerated interval of S: z0 from the share of resamples
  below the estimate, acceleration from the leave-one-out jackknife of S. It raises, and the
  simulation counts the failure, if every resample is on one side of the estimate (none occurred).
- **studentized**: with theta = log(sum B / sum A) and the delta-method standard error
  se = sd_i(B_i - R A_i) / (sqrt(n) mean(B)), each resample gives
  t* = (theta* - theta-hat)/se*, with se* the same formula on the resample. The interval for theta
  is [theta-hat - se q_0.95, theta-hat - se q_0.05] and is mapped back by S = 1 - exp(theta).
  The quantiles are order statistics ceil((B+1)p). A resample with zero standard error (one episode
  drawn n times) has t = +-infinity and is kept, so the interval can be unbounded at tiny n.

Checks (default suite): BCa against `scipy.stats.bootstrap(method="BCa")` on a skewed sample
(limits agree to 0.003 at 200,000 resamples); the studentized interval against a plain-loop
bootstrap-t on the same indices (agrees to 1e-10); the delta-method se against the bootstrap's own
standard deviation of the log ratio; B proportional to A gives a point interval for all three.

## 7. Why studentized is the default

The rule, stated here after the table above was seen (the pilot of 400 experiments per cell
pointed the same way, but the choice is not preregistered): the default must (a) meet the plan's
acceptance on all four empirical populations at both planning sizes, and (b) among the methods that
do, have the smallest largest false-exceedance rate over the empirical cells at all sizes, so that
a sample somewhat below the planned n is not a trap.

| | percentile | BCa | studentized |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) all 8 acceptance cells at most 0.06 | no (0.064) | yes | yes |
| (b) largest empirical rate over all 20 cells | 0.1165 (P1@60k, n = 40) | 0.0675 (P1@60k, n = 40) | 0.0545 (P1@60k, n = 1,713) |
| LN-heavy at n = 1,237 / 1,713 | 0.080 / 0.071 | 0.075 / 0.067 | 0.072 / 0.065 |

BCa passes (a) too, with the same worst cell, so the choice between BCa and studentized rests on (b)
and on the lognormals, where the studentized interval has the lowest rate in all ten cells (the
smallest margins over BCa, 0.0005 to 0.0015, are inside the Monte Carlo error). The studentized interval needs a positive total in arm B (it is a log ratio);
cost data have that.

Rejected: keeping the percentile interval (fails the bar as specified, and 0.117 at n = 40); a
correction by simulation inside the CLI (a fixed recipe is simpler and tested); a nested bootstrap
for se* (the delta-method se matches the bootstrap spread of the log ratio, and nesting would cost
two orders of magnitude of time).

## 10. What this does and does not show

- **Verified by running:** the rates, powers and widths above, from the committed seeds; the
  methods against scipy and a loop reference; the regeneration against `b1-variance.csv`.
- **Not shown.** (1) The selector. The costs of the selective arm are unknown; B1's proxy pairs
  are two baselines. A selector whose ratio distribution is heavier-tailed than P1@60k's or whose
  episodes are more skewed behaves more like the lognormals, where nothing meets 0.06 at the
  planning sizes. Re-run `a7b_calibrate.py` on paired costs from the selector against its baseline
  (the data file's columns are all it needs) before C3, as B2 recommended for q and sigma_1.
  (2) **The class mix.** The real design has a fixed number of seeds per class; the simulation
  draws pooled episodes, so its mix varies. The unstratified bootstrap in the CLI also ignores the
  strata. A fixed mix removes the between-class part of the variance that the bootstrap still
  includes, which should make the real size lower, not higher; this was not simulated.
  (3) **Dependence.** Episodes are treated as independent draws; episodes sharing a seed across
  classes were not checked (as in B2).
  (4) **The finite population.** The 5,500 pairs are a discrete population; at n = 1,713 about
  14% of the draws are repeats of episodes already in the sample. The true S of the null is the
  population's exactly, but a continuous cost distribution might behave a little differently.
  (5) **Planning sizes.** 1,237 and 1,713 are B2's sizes for the *success* criterion at a 2%
  discordant fraction; they are not EXP-001's frozen n. The default's size is at or below
  0.0545 at every n from 40 up on the empirical populations, so the freeze does not depend on the
  exact n for the *size* of this criterion; its power at n = 40 on P1@60k-like costs is poor (Table 5).
  (6) **Multiplicity / selection:** the default was chosen after seeing the 2,000-experiment table,
  and the extension on the worst cell is not independent of that look (it is, however, new
  experiments).
  (7) One-sided use of a two-sided 90% interval: the decision only uses the lower limit; the upper
  limit's coverage is not calibrated here.
- **The 0.06 acceptance is met as specified, but the statistic is noisy** (Table 2); it is the
  coordinator's call whether point estimates at 2,000 experiments suffice for the freeze or whether
  the planned-n cells should be repeated at 10,000 (about an hour per cell at n = 1,713 on one core).

## 11. Deviations and judgment calls

- The paired-cost table and the cell CSV are committed (360 KB and 25 KB). They are derived
  data, not the run directories (`artifacts/runs/` is not touched), and the simulation and the
  tests need them without the Rust binary. Remove them if the coordinator disagrees; the scripts
  regenerate both.
- Runs went through `scripts/cgroup-run.sh` directly rather than the driver (Section 3). The
  simulation itself ran under `cgroup-run.sh` too (core 3, one core, 2 GiB; peak 188 MB), one
  Python process at a time.
- Power used 2,000 resamples, not 10,000 (Section 8), with a 10,000-resample check.
- The lognormal parameters are mine (Section 4).
- `ratio_of_totals_ci` gains a `method` argument whose default is the studentized interval, so the
  CLI default and the library default change together. Two existing tests were adjusted, not
  weakened (see the commit message).
- With the studentized default a sample of n = 3 gives an unbounded lower limit (`-inf`, JSON null,
  a warning); this is by design and tested.

## 12. Reproduce

```bash
cargo build --locked --release -p gordian-run -j 3          # nothing else running
python3 experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_generate.py --verify-b1
scripts/cgroup-run.sh --name a7b-null --cpus 3 --cpu-quota 100 --memory 2G -- \
    analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py null          # ~2.3 h
scripts/cgroup-run.sh --name a7b-power --cpus 3 --cpu-quota 100 --memory 2G -- \
    analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py power --resamples 2000   # ~1 h
analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py null --resamples 2000 \
    --pops P1@60k LN-heavy --sizes 40 160 400                                                # Table 6
analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py power --resamples 10000 \
    --pops P1@60k --sizes 40 160 400
analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py null --first 2000 \
    --n-exp 8000 --pops P1@60k --sizes 1713                                                  # extension
analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py report
analysis/.venv/bin/python experiments/exploration/scripts/a7b_calibrate.py export
cd analysis && .venv/bin/python -W error -m pytest -q                    # default suite
cd analysis && .venv/bin/python -W error -m pytest -m slow -q            # acceptance cells
```

Results land in `$GORDIAN_WORK/a7b-results.jsonl` (default `/tmp/gordian-exploration-a7b/`); each
line carries the git revision, library versions, base seed and the paired-cost table's sha256
(`83e51917fab3ce40a9de291adb862dded0477f2443c2de001dcad4ac5e97ee21`). The stream of
`numpy.random.default_rng` is stable for a numpy version; a different numpy may change individual
rates within their Monte Carlo error.
